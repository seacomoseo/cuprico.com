You don't live in Spain full-time. Maybe you're not a Spanish tax resident at all. So can you still put solar panels on your villa in the Costa Blanca or the Region of Murcia? Yes — and if that question has been sitting at the back of your mind since you bought the place, this is worth reading before you get three different answers from three different installers.

We hear a version of this question from almost every non-resident client we work with, whether their villa is in [Los Alcázares](https://cuprico.com/en/solar-panel-installers/los-alcazares/), [Pilar de la Horadada](https://cuprico.com/en/solar-panel-installers/pilar-de-la-horadada/) or one of the golf resorts further inland: "I'm not here most of the year — can I actually do this, and who deals with it if I can't be?" Here's the honest, unglamorous answer, point by point.

## Do you need to be a Spanish resident to install solar?

No. What matters is that you **own the property** (or have the owner's authorisation) — not where you pay tax, and not how many days a year you spend in Spain. You can be a tax resident in the UK, Belgium, France or anywhere else and install a solar system on your Spanish villa on exactly the same terms as the neighbour who lives there year-round.

What does change, as a non-resident, is **who actually does the running around for you**. And that's where the real difference shows up between an installer who's used to working with owners who aren't there, and one who isn't.

## The one document that actually matters: your NIE

This is where most of the confusion comes from. You don't need a Spanish DNI (that's for Spanish nationals only), and you don't need tax residency — but you do need an **NIE** (Número de Identificación de Extranjero, Spain's foreigner ID number) for almost any serious paperwork here: signing the installation contract, registering as the account holder with the electricity distribution company, and — if a grant or rebate ever applies to your case — requesting it.

If you already bought your property in Spain, you almost certainly have an NIE already, since it's also required for the deed and for paying IBI (the Spanish equivalent of council tax). If you don't have one yet, you can request it at a Spanish consulate in your home country, or in Spain itself through the national police's foreigners' office. You don't need to be registered as a resident (*empadronado*) or a tax resident to hold one — an NIE identifies you, it doesn't grant residency.

## Your homeowners' association: do all your neighbours need to agree?

If your property is a standalone villa with its own roof, this doesn't apply to you at all. But if you're in a development with shared roof space or common areas, Spanish law makes this considerably easier than most owners assume: to install an individual renewable energy system — your own panels, even if they sit on a shared roof — **you don't need unanimous approval from the community**. Under the current wording of the Horizontal Property Law, it only takes a third of the owners, representing a third of the participation shares, to approve it.

In practice, that means you don't need to win over the whole street, and the community can't simply refuse outright. There are still details worth checking case by case — aesthetic rules some resorts apply, who pays for what if the installation uses shared elements — because the small print varies by development and the rules can be updated. We check what actually applies to your specific building before you commit to anything.

## Grants, rebates, and your tax status: what actually applies to you

This is the question where the most misinformation gets thrown around, so let's be straightforward about it:

- **Local council rebates (IBI, ICIO).** These are usually granted to whoever pays the property tax — the owner, resident or not. If your town hall currently has a rebate for self-consumption installations, you can normally apply for it on the same terms as a resident neighbour. Each council sets its own rules and you apply directly with them.
- **Regional grant schemes.** These depend on the specific call for applications in each autonomous community, and the requirements sometimes do — and sometimes don't — include Spanish tax residency. The large national scheme tied to the EU recovery plan (RD 477/2021, run through IDAE) is no longer active as of today, so treat any "40% grant" claim with real caution until you've checked the current, official position.
- **Income tax deductions (IRPF).** This one genuinely does depend on your tax status: it only applies if you file as a Spanish tax resident. If you're taxed as a non-resident (via Modelo 210), this specific deduction isn't the route for you — though it's still worth asking your own tax adviser whether anything else applies to your situation.

As with anything grant-related, the rules can change between one call for applications and the next. In your free assessment, we check what's actually open right now for your specific town and situation, rather than quoting you a generic figure that may not apply to you at all.

## Who actually picks up the phone if something goes wrong?

In our experience, this matters more than any paperwork question. It's not just "can I install this" — it's "what happens if something isn't right eight months from now, and I'm two thousand kilometres away?"

Ask any installer this directly before you sign anything: who is legally responsible if there's an issue, how do you reach them, and in what language will they actually explain the problem to you. A company with a fixed local address, in the area your property is in, with a phone number and a WhatsApp that someone genuinely answers — not a number that changes every year — is worth more than the cheapest quote sitting in your inbox.

On our side, when a client isn't in Spain during the works, we send photos and progress updates as the installation goes in, walk through and sign off on all the paperwork (the installation certificate or *boletín*, the contract with the distribution company, the warranty) over a video call if that's easier, and stay reachable on WhatsApp for whatever comes up after you've left.

## Keeping an eye on it from wherever you are

Almost every modern inverter comes with a monitoring app that shows exactly how much your system is producing and how much the house is using, in real time, from your phone. You don't need to be in Spain to confirm it's working properly — you can check it the same way you'd check a bank balance, from anywhere.

That connects to another question owners who are only here part of the year always ask: electricity your panels generate while the house sits empty isn't simply wasted, thanks to Spain's **surplus compensation scheme**, which credits what you feed into the grid against your next bill. And if your time in Spain grows over the years — as happened to one owner in [Pinar de Campoverde](https://cuprico.com/en/solar-panel-installers/pinar-de-campo-verde/), whose [real battery expansion case](https://cuprico.com/en/blog/ampliacion-bateria-pinar-de-campoverde/) we wrote up on the blog — the original system can always be reviewed and expanded later.

## A side effect most owners don't factor in: property value

Beyond the bill savings, a self-consumption installation improves a property's energy performance rating, and that increasingly matters if you ever decide to sell or rent the place out. For an owner who sees their Spanish property as an investment as much as a place to spend the summer, it's a point that rarely comes up in these conversations but is worth keeping in your back pocket.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to be a Spanish tax resident to install solar panels?**
No. You need to be the property owner (or have the owner's authorisation) and, in practice, an NIE to sign the contracts. Tax residency isn't a requirement for installing self-consumption.

**What if my homeowners' association doesn't want solar panels?**
For an individual renewable energy installation, unanimous approval isn't required — a third of the owners, representing a third of the participation shares, is enough. It's still worth checking your specific development's rules before installing.

**Can I access grants as a non-resident?**
It depends on the type of grant. Local council rebates (IBI, ICIO) are usually tied to property ownership, not residency. Income tax deductions do require filing as a resident. We confirm what applies to your specific case and municipality in your free assessment.

**Do I need to be in Spain during the installation?**
Not necessarily. We coordinate the installation, paperwork and commissioning by phone, WhatsApp or video call when you can't be here in person, and leave everything signed off and explained clearly.

**How do I know my system is working properly if I'm not in Spain?**
Through the inverter's monitoring app, which shows production and consumption in real time from any country.

**Will I need to speak Spanish to handle any of this?**
No. We handle the certificates, the registration with the distributor, and the paperwork directly, and we're happy to talk you through the whole process in English.
