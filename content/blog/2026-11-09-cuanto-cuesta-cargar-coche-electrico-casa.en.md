If you drive an electric car in Spain, or you're about to ship one over or buy one here, and you want to know what charging it at your villa really costs, this guide is for you. We won't quote you a figure from an advert: we'll give you the formula to work it out yourself with your own numbers, show you where to find them on a Spanish electricity bill, and explain what changes when you have solar panels on the roof.

## The formula: what it costs to charge your EV at home

You only need three figures:

**Charging cost = car consumption (kWh per 100 km) × kilometres driven ÷ 100 × price per kWh on your tariff**

An example with round numbers: if your car uses 16 kWh per 100 km and you drive 1,000 km a month, you need roughly 160 kWh a month. Multiply those 160 kWh by the price per kWh you pay, and that's your monthly charging cost.

One detail: a small share of energy is always lost while charging (in the charger, the cabling and the car's own battery). If you want to be precise, add a small margin to the result.

## Where to find each figure

- **Car consumption (kWh/100 km):** in the model's technical data, but the most reliable figure is your trip computer, because it reflects how you drive. It usually rises with air conditioning in summer and at motorway speeds.
- **Kilometres driven:** from the odometer over a normal month. If you're only here part of the year, calculate it for the months you're actually in Spain.
- **Price per kWh:** on your Spanish electricity bill, under the energy charge (*término de energía*). If your tariff has time periods, you'll see a different price for each one, and that's the key. If the bill is a mystery, [send it to us for a free review](https://cuprico.com/en/electricity-bill-review/).

## Why charging time matters

Almost every home in Spain is on the 2.0TD access tariff, where energy doesn't cost the same at every hour. According to the periods set by the [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/file/304517/download), the **off-peak period** (*valle*), the cheapest on time-of-use tariffs, runs from **0:00 to 8:00 every day** and covers **all of Saturday, Sunday and national holidays**.

For a car that sleeps at home, that's ideal: plug in when you arrive, the charger waits until midnight, and the car is ready in the morning. A charger like the **V2C Trydan** can be scheduled from your phone so it only charges in those hours, wherever you are.

If your contract has a single price at all hours, timing won't change the cost. Now that you're adding a car, it may be worth checking whether a time-of-use tariff would suit you better.

## Charging your EV with solar panels

This is where the numbers change. Energy your panels produce and you use straight away is **not bought from the grid**. Charge the car with it, and that part of the charge doesn't appear on your bill.

The charger can work in **solar surplus mode**: it reads how much spare energy your system has and adjusts the charging rate to match, instead of drawing from the grid. Energy you'd otherwise export at a low value ends up in your car.

The catch is obvious: to charge from the sun, the car has to be at home when the sun is out. That works well if:

::check:: You're retired or work from home and the car sits at the villa around midday.
::check:: You have a second car that stays at home during the week.
::check:: You do most of your charging at weekends.
::check:: You have a solar pergola over the parking space.

It works less well if the car leaves early and comes back after dark. Then the usual approach is a mix: whatever the sun can cover, and the rest in off-peak hours.

## What about a home battery?

A battery lets you store some daytime surplus and use it at night. Bear in mind, though, that a car battery is usually much larger than a home battery, so the home battery helps but won't normally fill the car on its own. The sensible approach is to design panels, battery and charger together, around your real routine. ## If the villa is a holiday home

A few practical points we often discuss with international owners:

- **Leaving the car while you're away.** Many owners keep an EV at their Spanish home. Check your manufacturer's advice on the ideal charge level for long periods parked, and set the charger accordingly from the app.
- **Charging during your stay.** If you're here in summer, when solar production is highest, the car can be charged largely from your panels during the day.
- **Shared garages.** In an apartment or townhouse community, a charger can usually be installed in your own parking space. There are steps to notify the community, which we explain and help with.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to charge an electric car at home in Spain?

It depends on your car's consumption, your mileage and the price per kWh on your tariff. Multiply the kWh you need by the price on your bill to get your real cost.

### When is it cheapest to charge?

In the off-peak period: 0:00 to 8:00 every day, plus all weekend and national holidays, if you're on a time-of-use tariff.

### Can I charge my car using only solar panels?

You can cover a good share of it if the car is at home when the sun is out. If it's used every working day, most owners combine solar and off-peak hours.
