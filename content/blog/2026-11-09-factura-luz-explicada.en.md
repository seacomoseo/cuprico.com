If you own a villa or holiday home in Spain and your electricity bill (*factura de la luz*) feels like a puzzle written in someone else's language, this guide is for you. Spanish bills are packed with terms that don't exist on a UK, Belgian or Scandinavian bill, and many owners only realise what they're paying for when a large bill arrives for a house that was empty most of the month.

Below we go through each line in plain English, explain how Spanish electricity tariffs and time periods work, and show where the money usually goes in a holiday home. Every time period and tax rate here comes from official sources (CNMC, BOE or the Spanish Tax Agency), linked as we go.

## The two main parts of your bill

### Power charge (*término de potencia*)

A fixed amount you pay for the kilowatts (kW) you have **contracted**, whether you use any electricity or not. Think of it as the size of the "pipe" into your house. This is why a holiday home still gets a bill when nobody is there: the power charge runs every day of the month.

For most homes, contracted power has **two periods**: a peak period (8:00 to 24:00 on weekdays) and an off-peak period (0:00 to 8:00 and all weekend and national holidays), and you can contract a different power for each, according to the [CNMC's guide to the electricity bill](https://www.cnmc.es/file/304517/download).

### Energy charge (*término de energía*)

What you pay for the kilowatt-hours (kWh) you actually use. This is the part that grows with air conditioning, the pool pump, a heat pump or an electric car, and the part solar panels reduce.

Both charges include the regulated network tolls and system charges (*peajes y cargos*), which are the same whichever supplier you choose.

## Time periods: when you use electricity matters

Almost every home in Spain is on the **2.0TD** access tariff, under which the price of energy changes during the day. The periods set by [CNMC Circular 3/2020](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/cir/2020/01/15/3) are:

| Period | Spanish name | Weekdays | Weekends and national holidays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peak | Punta (P1) | 10:00–14:00 and 18:00–22:00 | – |
| Standard | Llano (P2) | 8:00–10:00, 14:00–18:00 and 22:00–24:00 | – |
| Off-peak | Valle (P3) | 0:00–8:00 | All day |

In practical terms: running the washing machine, dishwasher or EV charger at night or at the weekend usually costs less than doing it at seven in the evening on a weekday. How big the difference is depends on your contract.

## The other lines you'll see

### Electricity tax (*impuesto eléctrico*)

A special tax calculated on the power and energy charges. Its general rate is **5.11269632%**, set in article 99 of [Law 38/1992 on Excise Duties](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/1992/12/28/38/con), as confirmed by the [Spanish Tax Agency](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/impuestos-especiales-medioambientales/impuesto-especial-sobre-electricidad/liquidacion-pago-impuesto/tipo-impositivo.html). In 2026 the government has approved temporary, conditional cuts to 0.5% for certain months, which only apply if electricity prices rise sharply. The [Tax Agency's 2026 updates](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/impuestos-especiales-medioambientales/novedades-impuestos-especiales-medioambientales/2026.html) show which months they have and haven't applied to.

### Meter rental (*alquiler de equipos de medida*)

A small regulated charge for the smart meter, if it belongs to the distribution company (as it usually does).

### VAT (*IVA*)

Charged on the total of the bill. The general rate is **21%**. During 2026, royal decree-laws have allowed a temporary reduction to 10% for contracts of up to 10 kW in certain months, again only if electricity prices pass a threshold. The latest, [Royal Decree-law 25/2026](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rdl/2026/09/29/25), provides for this in November and December 2026. Whether it applies in a given month depends on official price data, so check the VAT line on your own bill.

### Social bonus contribution (*financiación del bono social*)

A small line that funds the discounted tariff for vulnerable households. Every customer pays it.

## Regulated tariff or free market?

You can be on one of two types of contract:

- **Regulated tariff (PVPC).** Available only for contracts of up to 10 kW, under [Royal Decree 216/2014](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2014-3376). The energy price changes every hour, and since the reform in [Royal Decree 446/2023](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2023-14048), part of it is linked to longer-term market prices to make it less volatile. Only certain "reference suppliers" can offer it.
- **Free-market contract (*mercado libre*).** Any supplier, with a fixed price, a time-of-use price or other formulas set in your contract. Read the conditions: permanence clauses, extra services and how surplus solar is paid.

Neither is automatically cheaper. It depends on how and when you use electricity, and on the specific offer.

## Why your bill may be high in a holiday home

These are the most common causes we see in villas owned by international clients:

- **Contracted power higher than you need.** You pay for it every day, including all the months the house is empty.
- **The pool pump, irrigation or a dehumidifier** running in the evening peak while nobody is home.
- **Air conditioning used in the most expensive hours** during your stay.
- **A contract that no longer fits** how you use the house: perhaps chosen years ago, or inherited from the previous owner.
- **Estimated readings** or a contract still in the previous owner's name, which can cause problems with billing and paperwork.

## How solar helps (and what it can't change)

Solar panels cover part of your daytime use, which means fewer kWh bought from the grid: the energy part of the bill goes down. With a battery, you can also use your own solar in the evening peak.

What you don't use is exported. Under the **simplified surplus compensation** in [Royal Decree 244/2019](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2019-5089), your supplier credits it against that month's energy charge, depending on your contract. What solar does **not** reduce is the power charge, meter rental or the fixed part of taxes, so a well-designed system goes hand in hand with a contract that fits your real use. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I get an electricity bill when my house in Spain is empty?

Because of the power charge, meter rental and the taxes on them, which are paid every day regardless of use, plus whatever keeps running while you're away (fridge, pool pump, alarm).

### What are punta, llano and valle?

The three time periods of the 2.0TD tariff: peak, standard and off-peak. Off-peak (valle) covers 0:00 to 8:00 every day and all of Saturdays, Sundays and national holidays.

### Can I lower my contracted power?

Usually yes, but it has to be enough for everything that can run at the same time. Lowering it too far means the main switch trips. A qualified electrician should check your loads first.

### Is the regulated PVPC tariff better than a fixed price?

It depends on your use and on the offer. PVPC prices change every hour; a fixed-price contract gives predictability. Compare both with your real consumption.

### Is VAT on electricity 21% or 10%?

The general rate is 21%. In 2026 a reduced 10% rate has been possible in some months for contracts up to 10 kW, depending on official price data. Your bill shows which rate was applied.
