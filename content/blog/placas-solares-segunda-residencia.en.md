If you own a holiday home in Spain, a villa you only use part of the year (the summer, the winter months, or a few weeks here and there), and you're wondering whether solar panels make sense when the house sits empty for months, this is for you.

The short answer: **solar panels on a holiday home in Spain can pay their way**, but the system needs to be designed around how you actually use the property, not around a full-time household. Here's what matters, and what you should ask any installer before you sign.

## Do solar panels make sense on a holiday home in Spain?

It depends less on how many months you spend here and more on **what the house uses while you're away**, and on how the system is set up.

An "empty" villa on the coast rarely uses zero electricity. Typically, these keep running:

::check:: The pool pump, filtering all year round.
::check:: Fridge or freezer, router, alarm and security cameras.
::check:: A dehumidifier or air-con in dry mode, very common near the sea.
::check:: Irrigation, a well pump or programmed outdoor lighting.

Much of that load runs in daylight hours, exactly when the panels produce. That's your first saving, even when you're a thousand miles away. The second comes when you're here and using more: air-conditioning, heat pump, laundry, cooking, perhaps an electric car.

What we won't do is quote you a fixed saving figure without seeing your bills. Depending on your usage and tariff, savings can range from modest to substantial, and the only honest way to know is a study based on your real consumption. ## What happens to the electricity you produce while you're away?

This is the question every part-time owner asks. Whatever the house doesn't use is exported to the grid. For homes, the usual arrangement is Spain's **simplified surplus compensation** scheme, set out in [Royal Decree 244/2019](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2019-5089) (article 14). In plain English:

- Your electricity supplier credits the energy you export against your bill, at a price agreed with them (normally lower than the price you pay for energy you buy).
- The balance is settled **per billing period, which cannot exceed one month**.
- The credit **can never exceed the cost of the energy you bought that month**. The energy part of your bill can drop to zero, but not below.
- Fixed charges (contracted power, meter rental and the related taxes) **are not offset**. You pay those regardless.

What this means for a holiday home: in months when the house is closed and producing far more than it uses, **some of that surplus goes uncredited**. That's not a reason to avoid solar, but it's exactly why the system should be sized for your calendar.

Some Spanish suppliers offer a so-called "virtual battery", which banks the surplus that doesn't fit on your bill as a euro credit for later months. This is a commercial product, not part of RD 244/2019, so read the terms carefully (monthly fees, minimum contract periods, whether the credit expires). Regulations and supplier offers do change, so always check the details for your own case.

## A system built around your calendar, not an average household

The first thing we ask a holiday-home owner isn't "how big is your roof?" but **"when are you here?"**

**Summer visitors** coincide with peak production and peak demand (air-con, pool running flat out, a full house). It's the most rewarding scenario.

**Winter residents**, like many owners from the UK, Belgium or Scandinavia who escape the cold here, produce less per day, but the winter sun on the Costa Cálida and the Vega Baja still goes a long way. Running a heat pump during daylight hours makes a real difference.

**Occasional visitors** should avoid oversizing. More panels doesn't automatically mean more savings if half the year's output ends up as uncredited surplus.

With that in mind, we size the number of panels, choose the best orientation (many villas in [Pinar de Campoverde](https://cuprico.com/en/solar-panel-installers/pinar-de-campo-verde/) or golf resorts such as [Las Colinas](https://cuprico.com/en/solar-panel-installers/las-colinas-golf-and-country-club/) have several roof pitches or a pergola to work with), and decide whether a battery belongs in the plan now or later.

## Make the most of your surplus: loads worth scheduling

If the house will be empty for long spells, the smart move is to shift whatever you can to daylight hours:

- **Pool pump** running at midday instead of overnight.
- **Electric water heater or heat pump** heating with solar.
- **Dehumidifying or ventilation** during production hours.
- **Irrigation** pumps, also in daylight.

We set these schedules up at commissioning (timers, inverter settings or the equipment's own programming) so you don't need to fiddle with anything from abroad.

## And a battery? Who it suits and who it doesn't

With the house empty, a battery charges by day and barely discharges at night, so **its financial return drops**. That doesn't mean it never makes sense:

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I lose the electricity my panels produce while I'm away?

Not all of it. Whatever the house is using at the time (pool pump, fridge, alarm) comes from the panels. The rest is exported and, under simplified compensation, credited against that month's bill until the energy charge reaches zero. Anything beyond that isn't credited, unless your supplier offers some form of "virtual battery".

### Can I monitor my system from the UK or elsewhere?

Yes. The inverter's app shows production, consumption, exports and fault alerts. We set it up in your name and can check it remotely if you ask us to.

### Is a battery worth it if I only come in summer?

In most cases, not in the short term. It usually makes more sense to install a hybrid-ready inverter and add a battery if you start spending more time here or want backup during power cuts.

### Is it safe to leave the system running in an empty house?

Yes, provided it's properly installed with the right protection. We recommend periodic inspections, especially near the sea. Never open the consumer unit or inverter yourself.

### Do I need to be in Spain for the installation?

No. We coordinate the survey, installation and paperwork by phone, email or WhatsApp. If you're not a Spanish resident, everything you need to know is in our [non-resident's guide to going solar in Spain](https://cuprico.com/en/blog/placas-solares-no-residente-espana/).
